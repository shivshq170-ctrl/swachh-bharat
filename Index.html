01 • Look around
The dirty side of the story

Littering, mixed waste, blocked drains and careless disposal can make public spaces unhealthy and unpleasant. The causes are usually ordinary habits — which means ordinary people can change them too.

Plastic everywhere

Single-use plastic can end up in streets, drains and waterways when it is thrown away carelessly.

🧴 Bin says: “I have a mouth. Please use it.”

Waste without a plan

When wet and dry waste are mixed, useful material becomes harder to recover and recycle.

🍌 Banana peel + newspaper = not a science experiment. Segregate!

Neglected public spaces

Dirty corners can become “normal” when everyone assumes somebody else is responsible.

🧹 Plot twist: “Somebody” is actually all of us.
02 • Before → After
Small action. Big transformation.

Cleanliness drives work best when they are followed by habits that keep the place clean. Here is the basic idea: identify the problem, remove the waste, segregate it, and prevent the mess from returning.

BEFORE • THE MESS

“I'll put it here for now.”

And then “for now” becomes three months. 🙃

AFTER • THE CHANGE

“Let's keep it this way.”

Clean surroundings are easier to maintain when everyone plays a part. 🌿

04 • What's happening now
Latest government cleanliness programmes

These highlights use official government information available in September 2026.

2026 • Swachhata Hi Seva

Swachhata Mein Sahbhag

The 2026 campaign began from 17 September and focuses on citizen participation, clean public places, youth engagement, SafaiMitra support, source segregation and “Waste to Wealth”.

1 October 2026

Ek Din, Ek Ghanta, Ek Saath

A nationwide one-hour voluntary cleanliness activity is planned for 8–9 AM, bringing citizens together for shramdaan.

2 October 2026

Swachh Bharat Diwas

Special Gram Sabhas and urban council meetings are planned, alongside plantation drives and a voluntary Swachhata Pledge through MyGov.

SBM(G) Phase II

Rural waste & greywater management

Government reporting says 5,63,809 of 5,86,944 villages had been covered with liquid-waste-management arrangements as of 7 August 2026.
